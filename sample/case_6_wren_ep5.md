# Scenario: Wren (Episode 5)

**Prompt:** Wren tries to work out why Kamala has been so distracted lately.

## Permitted Facts (Narrative Boundary)
- Kamala announces to the assembled company that the season will close with a revival of The Winter Boarders, with all four company members present.
- Wren cries on the hamper in the costume store after having her scene cut, and Priya sits with her on the floor without saying much, telling no one about it afterwards.
- Dev misses an entire day of rehearsal without offering an explanation, and Kamala chooses not to press him about it.
- Priya quietly rewrites the understudy plot so Wren covers Dev's role without clearing it with Kamala, and instructs Wren to start learning the part.
- The lighting desk fails halfway through the second act, forcing Kamala, Dev, Priya, and Wren to stay past midnight. Tomas is not present and is never told.
- Kamala puts Wren on in Dev's part during the dress rehearsal rather than lose the run, and Wren performs extraordinarily for two and a half hours.

## Generated Scene
The hamper’s edge scraped against Wren’s knee as she wiped her face with the back of her hand—stale cotton and sweat mingling. Outside the costume shop window, the city lights bled into the wet pavement, but inside, Kamala’s absence was a heavier thing than the silence.  

Kamala pushed open the door, her coat collar turned up against the cold, and stopped short. Her hands were shaking slightly as she traced the edge of a costume rack—Dev’s winter coat, now hanging where it shouldn’t. “You look… like you’ve been crying,” Kamala said, her voice low, almost a whisper.  

Wren didn’t look up. “I did.”  

Kamala’s eyes darted to the floor, then back to Wren. “Dev didn’t come to rehearsal today.”  

Wren’s breath hitched. She’d known that. *Knew* it. But Kamala’s words landed like a stone in the quiet. “He didn’t say anything,” Wren said, her voice tight.  

Kamala’s fingers tightened on the coat hanger. For a second, Wren saw the tremor in her knuckles, the way her eyes flicked past Wren to the empty space where Dev should’ve stood. Then Kamala turned her head away, her jaw clenched.  

“Priya told me to tell you to start learning his lines,” Kamala said finally, her voice strained. “But… I don’t know if I can do this.”  

Wren’s hand went to her chest. The words hit her like a fist—*Priya reworked the plot without telling Kamala*. She hadn’t even asked why Kamala was so quiet since the dress rehearsal. Now she saw it: Kamala’s distraction wasn’t about missing Dev. It was about *her*.  

Kamala’s gaze snapped back to Wren. “You’ll be fine,” she said, but her eyes were wide, desperate.  

Wren reached out, not to touch, but to hold the space between them. “You’ll be fine,” she repeated, softer this time. And for the first time, Kamala didn’t flinch.  

The hamper’s edge pressed against Wren’s leg. The city lights outside were gone now—just the quiet. And Wren understood: Kamala’s distraction wasn’t about Dev. It was about her.
