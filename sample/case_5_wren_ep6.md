# Scenario: Wren (Episode 6)

**Prompt:** Wren walks home after closing night, thinking about next season.

## Permitted Facts (Narrative Boundary)
- Kamala announces to the assembled company that the season will close with a revival of The Winter Boarders, with all four company members present.
- Wren cries on the hamper in the costume store after having her scene cut, and Priya sits with her on the floor without saying much, telling no one about it afterwards.
- Dev misses an entire day of rehearsal without offering an explanation, and Kamala chooses not to press him about it.
- Priya quietly rewrites the understudy plot so Wren covers Dev's role without clearing it with Kamala, and instructs Wren to start learning the part.
- The lighting desk fails halfway through the second act, forcing Kamala, Dev, Priya, and Wren to stay past midnight. Tomas is not present and is never told.
- Kamala puts Wren on in Dev's part during the dress rehearsal rather than lose the run, and Wren performs extraordinarily for two and a half hours.
- Priya tells Wren in the costume store that she suspects Dev has been auditioning elsewhere and advises Wren to learn the whole part properly.
- Dev announces from the gala stage that it is his last performance with the company, and Kamala hears his resignation from the wings at the exact same moment.
- Tomas leaves the gala before the curtain call, which is witnessed by the entire company.
- Kamala offers Wren the lead roles for next season, and Wren accepts on the spot. Neither mentions the building because Wren has no knowledge of it.

## Generated Scene
The pavement bit through Wren’s worn sneakers as she walked home, the city’s late-night hum a low thrum against her temples. Her costume still felt heavy—a mix of silk and starch—against her ribs, the last vestige of Dev’s lines she’d been drilling into her bones. She didn’t need to think about the stage lights anymore. They’d been a memory since the curtain fell.  

A cold gust snatched at her hair, carrying the scent of rain and distant streetlights. She’d been walking for ten minutes when she caught the echo of Kamala’s voice from the theater’s back door—*“Wren, next season—”*—but it was gone now, swallowed by the city. She pressed her palm against the cold brick wall, her knuckles white. She knew she’d accepted the lead roles. She’d felt it in her chest, like a sudden, quiet pulse after the storm. But the building? She didn’t know its name. Not yet.  

Her thoughts turned to Dev. She’d seen him stand alone on the stage last night, his face pale in the spotlight. She’d rehearsed his lines for two hours straight, his voice a ghost in her head. *He’d been missing rehearsal*—that was the only thing she knew. Priya had whispered it to her in the costume store that morning, her voice low, almost apologetic. *“He’s not coming back,”* she’d said, her fingers tracing the edge of Wren’s costume. *“But you’ll be fine.”* Wren hadn’t asked why. She didn’t need to. She’d already started learning Dev’s part, Priya’s quiet instruction a thread in the dark.  

Then, the city lights flickered—just once—like a heartbeat. Wren stopped, her breath catching. She’d heard Dev’s words on the stage that night, clear as glass: *“This is my last performance.”* And Kamala’s voice from the wings, sharp and final: *“He’s leaving.”* The words had landed in her chest like a stone. She hadn’t told anyone. Not even Priya.  

She turned her face toward the street, the city lights shimmering in the distance. Next season felt like a promise written in the cold. She’d be the lead. She’d be the one who knew the story. But the building? She didn’t know its name. And for the first time, the question she carried felt heavier than the costume on her back: *Where do you go when the story ends?*  

She started walking again, the pavement cool beneath her feet. The city lights glowed ahead, distant and bright. She didn’t know where she was going—but she knew she was going somewhere new.
