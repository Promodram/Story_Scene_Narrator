# Scenario: Kamala (Episode 5)

**Prompt:** Kamala confronts Dev in the empty auditorium about whether he still wants to be here.

## Permitted Facts (Narrative Boundary)
- Kamala announces to the assembled company that the season will close with a revival of The Winter Boarders, with all four company members present.
- Tomas tells Kamala the lease expires in 11 months and will not renew automatically.
- Tomas warns Kamala that the lease expires in eleven months and will not renew automatically, and Kamala tells no one about the conversation.
- Kamala briskly cuts Wren's only scene in front of Dev and Priya
- Kamala finds the 2019 arts council grant file and locks it into her desk drawer alone.
- 14 years ago, Kamala forged her missing co-founder's signature on the roof grant application.
- Dev misses an entire day of rehearsal without offering an explanation, and Kamala chooses not to press him about it.
- Kamala watches Tomas and Priya speaking in the car park from the fire escape; she hears nothing and concludes Tomas is going behind her back.
- Tomas describes the tenancy as 'under review' at the board meeting and quickly moves on.
- Kamala asks Dev to headline a closing-night fundraising gala; Dev agrees ambiguously.
- The lighting desk fails halfway through the second act, forcing Kamala, Dev, Priya, and Wren to stay past midnight. Tomas is not present and is never told.
- Kamala puts Wren on in Dev's part during the dress rehearsal rather than lose the run, and Wren performs extraordinarily for two and a half hours.
- Kamala admits to herself that Wren is better in the part than Dev, but keeps this observation entirely private.

## Generated Scene
The dust motes dance in the single shaft of moonlight cutting through the high window. Kamala’s bare feet press into the cold tile of the empty auditorium floor—*her* floor, the one she’s known since before she could walk. The lighting desk’s final failure echoes in her skull like a dropped hinge. She didn’t call for the crew. Didn’t need to. The silence here is the loudest thing in the world.  

She doesn’t move until she hears the door click. Dev stands in the shadows beyond the stage, his shadow stretched long across the proscenium. His eyes are fixed on the empty stage, not on her. *He thinks he’s the only one who knows*, she thinks, the thought sharp as glass. *He thinks he’s the only one who knows what’s left.*  

"Dev," she says, her voice low but carrying the weight of a decision made in the dark. "You still want to be here?"  

He doesn’t flinch. "I don’t know what you mean."  

"Last night," she says, stepping closer. The air smells of stale coffee and old wood. "The lights went out. We stayed past midnight. Tomas wasn’t there. He never told anyone."  

Dev’s jaw tightens. "I didn’t know."  

"Then you weren’t there," she says, her voice cracking. "You weren’t there when Wren did Dev’s part. He did it for two and a half hours. And he’s better."  

Dev flinches. "Wren?"  

"Yes," she says. "He’s better." She doesn’t look at him. "You think you’re the only one who knows how much time we have left."  

He stares at her. "I don’t know what you’re talking about."  

She turns to face him, her breath fogging the cold air. "The lease expires in eleven months," she says, the words heavy as stone. "Tomas says it’s ‘under review.’ But it’s not. It’s over. And I locked the grant file away—*my* grant file—because I know what that means."  

Dev’s eyes widen. He doesn’t speak.  

Kamala runs a hand over the edge of the proscenium, her fingers tracing the cold metal. She doesn’t tell him about the car park, the missing rehearsal day, the way she watched Tomas and Priya speak. She doesn’t need to. The silence between them is the only truth left: *he doesn’t know the lease is over*.  

And she doesn’t know if he’s the only one who doesn’t know.  

The moonlight fades. The only sound is her own heartbeat.  

"Eleven months," she says, her voice barely a whisper. "Eleven months."
